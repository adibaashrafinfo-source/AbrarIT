Prepared: 2026-11-28 · Status: v1 draft, ready for founder review

## 1. Executive Summary

Abrar IT has, in the last few days, gone from an unindexed website to a
technically excellent one: 21 pages live, full schema markup, 8
keyword-targeted service pages, 6 blog posts, Google Search Console
verified with sitemap submitted successfully, and an AI-crawler-friendly
`robots.txt`. **The site is now ready to be found. It is not yet being
looked for.** Nobody searches "Abrar IT" today, no other website links to
it, and it has no social presence a prospect could stumble into. The next
12 months are about closing that gap — turning a well-built storefront
into a known name in Bangladesh's digital-agency market.

**Three big bets for this plan:**

1. **Win the "recurring retainer," not just the "one-off project."**
   Website and branding work (what the current content emphasizes) is a
   *large but infrequent* need — a business redesigns its site once every
   2-3 years. Digital marketing, content and email/SMS management are a
   *large and frequent* need — the kind of problem that, once sold,
   compounds into monthly recurring revenue and repeat word-of-mouth.
   This plan treats one-off services as the acquisition wedge and
   retainer services as the real growth engine.
2. **Turn 9 real clients into Abrar IT's first distribution channel**
   before spending a single taka chasing strangers. Backlink credits,
   testimonials, referrals and case studies from Ramani Collection,
   NextGen Properties, Career Bridge BD and the other 6 real projects
   are higher-trust and lower-cost than any cold-acquisition channel
   available right now.
3. **Be citable, not just rankable.** Google ranking takes months.
   Showing up in ChatGPT/Perplexity answers and Facebook group
   recommendations can happen in weeks, and runs on different inputs
   (directory listings, genuine mentions, a real social presence) than
   classic SEO does. This plan runs both tracks in parallel rather than
   waiting for one to finish before starting the other.

**90-day priorities** (detail in §9): (1) ship the off-page kit that
already exists but hasn't been executed — backlink asks, Google
Business Profile, 3 directory listings; (2) stand up one real,
consistently-posted social channel (Facebook, since that's where
Bangladeshi SMB buyers actually are) rather than a thin presence on
four; (3) convert the CMS from "built" to "live" so the blog can
actually be updated without a code deploy; (4) get the first 3 genuine
client testimonials/reviews so real `AggregateRating` schema and
social proof become possible.

**12-month outcome this plan is built to produce:** Abrar IT ranks on
page 1 for at least 3 of its "[service] in Bangladesh" keywords,
appears in at least 2 AI-chatbot answers for category queries, holds a
real (not placeholder) social presence with a posting rhythm, and has
converted at least 2 of its 9 existing clients into retainer
relationships — proving the retainer model before scaling acquisition
spend toward it.

## 2. Strategic Frame

**Category claim:** Abrar IT is a full-service digital agency for
Bangladeshi SMBs who need to look and perform like an international
brand without hiring an in-house team — one partner for branding,
website, software and the ongoing marketing that keeps all three
working.

**ICP (distilled from the 9 real client projects already in
portfolio):** A Bangladeshi small-to-mid business owner or marketing
lead, already generating revenue offline or through a weak existing
digital presence, across these observed segments: e-commerce/retail
brands (Ramani Collection, Ruposhee), real estate developers (NextGen
Properties, Shahada Properties), hospitality/investment projects
(Sylvan Wye Resort), corporate services firms (Mohinur Business
Solutions, Trax Designers), recruitment platforms (Career Bridge BD),
and education providers (BD Online Quran Academy). **This is a wide
ICP for a young agency** — a reasonable strategic question for the
founder is whether to keep serving all six verticals or declare one
or two as the specialty to be known for (see §13, open decision #1).

**Business-model logic:** Project-based service revenue today
(one-off branding/web/software fees, ৳300–৳3,000+ starting prices per
the live pricing). The compounding opportunity is converting project
clients into **retainer clients** for digital marketing, content and
email/SMS management — services that are inherently recurring but
currently sold and delivered as if they were one-off, per the current
site structure.

**Brand voice non-negotiables** (extracted from the site's own copy,
which this plan treats as the already-established voice):
- **Premium without being inaccessible** — "world-class," "premium,"
  "award-worthy" language paired with transparent, published starting
  prices (a deliberate and good choice — most "premium" agencies hide
  pricing; Abrar IT's site doesn't).
- **Specific over vague** — the site already favors "300+ projects,
  500+ clients" over "many happy clients." Marketing content should
  keep this habit; it's also exactly what gets content cited by AI
  answer engines (see `SEO-KEYWORDS.md` / `SEO.md`).
- **Bangladesh-proud, globally-benchmarked** — positioned as a
  Dhaka-based agency building to international standard, not an
  international agency that happens to have Bangladeshi clients. Keep
  this frame in every social and outreach asset.

**Market-quality gate:**

| | Low frequency | High frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Large problem** | Website/branding (redesign every 2-3 yrs) — *expensive to stay top-of-mind; needs strong always-on SEO/content so Abrar IT is found the moment the need strikes* | **Digital marketing, content, email/SMS management — build here.** Monthly recurring need, compounding relationship |
| **Small problem** | — | — |

Abrar IT's current site content and pricing structure markets all 8
services as equally weighted, one-off-feeling line items. The
strategic correction this plan makes: treat web/branding/software as
the **acquisition wedge** (what gets a stranger to raise their hand)
and treat marketing/content/email as the **retention and revenue
engine** (what turns a one-time client into a compounding relationship).
This single reframe should influence pricing page ordering, sales
conversations, and which case studies get produced first.

## 5. Activation — From Aware to First Deal

For a service agency, "activation" is the path from *stranger noticed
us* to *stranger became a client*. Today that path is: find the site
→ read a service page → click "Get a Quote" / WhatsApp → message → (manual,
founder-led) conversation → proposal → signed project.

**What's working:** The site already does the hard part well — clear
pricing, clear CTAs, a WhatsApp button for the low-friction channel
Bangladeshi buyers actually prefer over email. This is a real strength;
don't redesign it.

**Gaps:**
- **No lead capture before the "ready to buy" moment.** Someone reading
  a blog post who isn't ready to hire yet has no way to stay in touch —
  there's a newsletter box in the footer with no backend (confirmed in
  §3). This is a leaky top-of-funnel: interested-but-not-ready visitors
  leave and never return.
- **No speed-to-lead tracking.** WhatsApp/contact form inquiries go
  straight to founder's phone/email with no record of response time —
  worth tracking informally (a simple spreadsheet: inquiry time → first
  response time → outcome) since response speed is one of the
  highest-leverage, zero-cost conversion levers for a service business.
- **Contact form submissions currently go nowhere** until the CMS is
  deployed to real hosting (per `cms/README.md`) — this is a direct
  acquisition leak: the contact form has been live and collecting
  nothing actionable.

**90-day moves:**
1. **Deploy the CMS to real PHP/MySQL hosting** (this is the single
   highest-priority item in this entire plan that isn't already
   SEO-related — every other acquisition effort is wasted if an
   interested lead's form submission has nowhere to land).
2. Wire the footer newsletter signup to something real — at minimum,
   forward signups to the founder's email via the CMS's existing
   `contact_messages` table (same mechanism, different source label) so
   it's not dead UI.
3. Track inquiry → first-response time informally for 90 days to
   establish a baseline.

## 6. Retention — Turning One Project Into a Relationship

This is where the "big bet #1" from §1 lives. Today, nothing in the
sales or delivery process appears designed to convert a one-off client
into a retainer client.

**90-day moves:**
1. At the delivery/handoff of every active or near-future project, add
   one explicit conversation: *"Now that your site/brand is live, do
   you want help keeping it performing?"* — positioning digital
   marketing, content, or email/SMS management as the natural next
   step, not a separate sales cycle.
2. Create one simple **retainer package** (even if informally priced at
   first) bundling: monthly content writing + email/SMS management +
   basic performance reporting. This directly operationalizes the
   market-quality-gate insight from §2.
3. Light-touch check-ins with past/current clients at 30/90 days
   post-launch — a WhatsApp message asking how the site/campaign is
   performing. Cheap, relationship-building, and a natural lead-in to
   the retainer pitch and the review/backlink ask (§4) in the same
   conversation.

**12-month move:** once 2-3 retainer relationships exist, write one
of them up as a case study — "From Website to Growth Partner" — this
becomes a cornerstone content asset per the "one asset, many returns"
principle (feeds the blog, a LinkedIn post, a GBP update, and a sales
conversation with the next prospect).

## 7. Referral — Clients Bringing Clients

No formal mechanism exists today. Given the ICP is business owners who
know other business owners, this is underused.

**90-day moves:**
1. The backlink/testimonial outreach already in motion (§4) doubles as
   a referral-priming conversation — a client who agrees to a
   testimonial has just told you they're happy, which is exactly the
   moment to ask "do you know another business that could use this?"
2. No formal referral *commission* program yet — too early without
   retainer revenue to fund it (see §13 open decision).

**12-month move:** once 2+ retainer clients exist and cash flow
supports it, introduce a simple referral incentive (e.g., one free
month of a retainer service, or a flat referral fee) for any client
who refers a signed project.

## 8. Revenue — Pricing, Packaging, Positioning

Current pricing (live on `services.html`) is transparent and
well-structured for one-off project sales:

| Service | Starting price |
|---|---|
| Content Writing | ৳300 |
| Graphic Design | ৳500 |
| Video Editing | ৳1,000 |
| Motion Graphics | ৳1,500 |
| Email & SMS Marketing | ৳1,500 |
| Digital Marketing | ৳2,000 |
| Website Design | ৳3,000 |
| Software Solutions | ৳3,000 |

Plus the 3-tier package structure on `services.html` (Starter ৳2,500 /
Growth ৳7,500 / Enterprise custom).

**Observations:**
- Pricing transparency is a genuine competitive advantage — keep it.
  Most competing agencies hide pricing behind "contact us," which also
  actively hurts them with AI shopping agents (per the `ai-seo`
  skill's agent-readiness guidance) that skip opaque pricing entirely.
- **No recurring/retainer pricing tier exists yet.** The package
  structure (Starter/Growth/Enterprise) is itself one-off-project
  framed ("per project").
- **No upsell path is visible on the pricing page itself** from a
  single service (e.g., Website Design) toward the bundle.

**90-day move:** add one retainer-framed package to the existing
pricing page — e.g., "Growth Partner — ৳X/month" bundling content +
email/SMS + light reporting — directly priced and visible, not just a
sales-conversation ask. This also gives GSC/AI engines a crawlable page
to cite for "digital marketing retainer Bangladesh"-type queries.

**12-month move:** once real retainer clients exist, introduce a
second, higher tier once there's a track record to justify it (avoid
inventing tiers with no delivered proof behind them).

## 10. 12-Month Outlook

Framed around **revenue/traction milestones**, not funding rounds
(none apply here) — each milestone unlocks the next tier of spend/effort.

| Milestone | What it unlocks |
|---|---|
| **First 10 inbound leads/month from organic + social (no paid spend)** | Confidence to introduce a small paid-social test budget (৳5,000-10,000/month) behind the best-performing organic content, not blind spend |
| **First 2 retainer clients signed** | Proof the retainer model works; formalize the package, consider a dedicated landing page for it |
| **First 20 real Google reviews** | Add genuine `AggregateRating` schema (deliberately withheld until now, per `SEO.md`) |
| **Page-1 Google ranking for 1+ "[service] in Bangladesh" keyword** | Signal to double down on that specific service page's content depth and internal linking |
| **First AI-chatbot citation confirmed** | Validates the off-page/directory strategy; expand to more directories and PR outreach |
| **Consistent founder bandwidth strain (can't keep up with posting + client work)** | Time to consider the first marketing hire — a generalist who can own content + social execution, freeing the founder for strategy and sales (see §13) |

This plan deliberately does **not** project SaaS-style exponential growth
or a specific ARR figure — there is no historical CAC or conversion
data to forecast from yet (§13, open decision #2). The honest 12-month
goal is: *go from zero external visibility to a small, provable,
repeatable lead-generation motion*, which is the correct first S-curve
for this stage.

   one search niche. Consider: is there one vertical (e-commerce?
   real estate?) worth being *known for* first, even while serving
   others?
2. **CAC is currently unknown** — there is no historical data on what
   it costs to acquire a client through any channel, because no
   tracked acquisition has happened yet. Every revenue projection in
   this plan is directional until GA4 + 90 days of data exist. This is
   the single highest-impact unknown in the plan, as it should be at
   this stage.
3. **Real team size and marketing bandwidth:** the website states "20+
   team members," but this engagement has been run solo by the
   founder. If there genuinely is a larger team, parts of this plan
   (e.g., social posting cadence, case-study production) can move
   faster than assumed — worth clarifying so the 90-day roadmap isn't
   under- or over-scoped.
4. **Marketing budget:** not yet stated. This plan assumes near-zero
   cash spend for 90 days (§11). If there is in fact budget available,
   the paid-social and paid-search timelines in §4 and §10 can move
   up.
5. **Retainer pricing:** §8 recommends adding a retainer package but
   doesn't set a specific price — that needs the founder's judgment on
   margin and market rate, informed by the first 1-2 real retainer
   conversations.

### A note on scope versus the standard 13-section template

This plan intentionally compresses or reframes sections of the
standard SaaS-oriented template that don't fit a bootstrapped,
founder-led Bangladeshi service agency: no funding-round-based
capability unlocks (§10 uses revenue/traction milestones instead), no
ARR/LTV/CAC-formula budget math (§11 uses a flat bootstrapped posture
with explicit gates), and no π-shaped-hire framework (§13 names the
*question* of a first hire without prescribing a title this stage
doesn't need yet). Where the standard template calls for a number this
engagement doesn't have, that's listed above as an open decision
rather than invented.
